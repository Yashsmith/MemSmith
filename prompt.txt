Your improved 2 minute script

I'd present it like this:

> "Let me make this concrete with one customer."

Imagine we have a customer who, today, still looks completely active.

They're using Neo. They're logging in. Nothing on the latest quarter's data tells us that we've lost them.

But what if they're actually on their way out?

That's the question we wanted the system to answer.

Because the problem with looking at just the latest quarter is that you're looking at a snapshot. And a snapshot can tell you where the customer is today, but not where they're heading.

So instead, we look backwards and reconstruct the customer's behavioural journey across multiple quarters.

And that's where the interesting part begins.

We don't just ask, "How much did this customer use Neo?"

We ask a series of more meaningful questions.

How recently did they engage? That's our Recency signal.

Was that drop in activity just temporary, or has it persisted?

Is their engagement actually declining over time, or are they recovering? That's where Momentum becomes important.

Are they still using Neo broadly, or are they slowly abandoning different capabilities?

And finally, is this behaviour actually unusual for this customer, or is this simply how they normally behave?

Put those signals together, and we get something much richer than a simple count of sessions or clicks.

Now we can give that behavioural profile to multiple models, such as Logistic Regression, XGBoost and LightGBM, and ask them to identify patterns associated with future inactivity.

But a prediction alone isn't enough.

If the system tells us, "This customer is high risk," the obvious next question is: why?

That's where Explainable AI comes in.

It allows us to go from "this customer is at risk" to "these specific changes in their behaviour are what drove that prediction."

So ultimately, we're not just predicting who might become inactive.

We're reconstructing the behavioural path that tells us why they're getting there.

And that's what turns raw usage history into behavioural intelligence."
